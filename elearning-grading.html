Grade with Confidence in Brightspace

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Choose a grading system and final grade settings that match your course outline
  • Add a published rubric to an assignment
  • Find the quiz settings that control attempts, results and the grade book

Lesson 1 of 4 · About 8 minutes

Grading Principles

Before you grade anything, decide how the course will add up. These choices are made once in the grade book setup and shape every grade that follows.

Choose a Grading System

Brightspace offers three grading systems. You pick one when you set up the grade book.

SystemHow the final grade worksUse it when
WeightedEach category is worth a set percentage of the final grade, and the categories add up to 100%. Each item is weighted within its category.Your course outline says things like "quizzes are worth 20%".
PointsTotal points earned divided by total points possible. An item's maximum points set how much it counts.Your course simply adds up points.
FormulaPoints, plus a custom formula for special rules, such as a minimum score on the final exam.You need rules the other two cannot express. It may not be turned on at every organization.

Decide What the Learner Sees

Calculated final grade
Brightspace works out the grade from the items and their weights or points.
Adjusted final grade
Starts from the calculated grade, but you can change it before you release it.

Decide How to Treat Ungraded Work

Drop ungraded items
Work without a score is left out, so the grade shows how the learner is doing on what has been graded so far.
Treat ungraded items as 0
Work without a score counts as zero, so the grade shows where the learner would land if nothing else came in.

Lesson 2 of 4 · About 8 minutes

Inserting Rubrics

A rubric tells learners what good work looks like before they start, and keeps your marking consistent across every submission.

Two Kinds of Rubric

Analytic
Several criteria, each scored on its own, such as Argument, Evidence and Writing. Use it when you want to show learners exactly where they lost marks.
Holistic
One criterion that judges the work as a whole. Use it for quick, overall judgements, such as a pass or redo on a short reflection.

Three Ways to Score

  • No Score: levels with descriptions only. Good for feedback without a grade.
  • Points: every criterion uses the same point values for each level.
  • Custom Points: each criterion can carry different point values, so one criterion can count more than another.

Status Decides Whether You Can Use It

Draft

Still being written. It cannot be added to new activities yet.

Published

Ready. You can add it to assignments and other activities.

Archived

Retired. It stays in past evaluations but cannot be added again.

Try It: Put the Steps in Order

These are the steps for adding an existing rubric to an assignment, but they are shuffled. Use the Move up and Move down buttons to put them in order, then check.

  1. Select "Add Rubric", then "Add Existing"
  2. Open the Assignments tool
  3. Select "Save and Close"
  4. Expand "Evaluation & Feedback"
  5. Open the assignment's menu and choose "Edit Assignment"
  6. Tick the rubric and select "Add Selected"

Lesson 3 of 4 · About 8 minutes

Quizzes

A quiz can mark itself and send the score straight to the grade book. Most of the work is choosing the right settings before learners start.

Build the Quiz

  • In the Quizzes tool, select New Quiz and give it a name.
  • Use Add/Edit Questions to write new questions or bring in questions from the Question Library, where you can reuse them across quizzes.
  • Link the quiz to a Grade item, either an existing one or a new one, so its score has a place in the grade book.

Where the Settings Live

The quiz settings are grouped into panels down the side of the quiz editor:

PanelWhat you set there
Availability Dates & ConditionsStart and end dates, release conditions, a password, and special access for individual learners
Timing & DisplayTime limits, questions per page, and shuffling
Attempts & CompletionHow many attempts learners get and how the overall grade is worked out across attempts
Evaluation & FeedbackAuto-publishing attempt results, syncing to the grade book, and what learners see after they submit

Lesson 4 of 4 · About 6 minutes

Grades

With the grade book set up, entering and releasing grades is routine. The care goes into checking before learners can see anything.

Three Ways to Enter Grades

  • One learner: open a learner and enter all their grades. Good for a learner who handed everything in late.
  • One grade item: open an item and enter it for the whole class. Good right after a test.
  • Spreadsheet View: type numbers straight into a grid. Good for entering a lot of grades at once.

Handle Missing and Excused Work on Purpose

  • Exemptions excuse a learner from an item, so it does not count for or against them.
  • Automatic zeros can fill in a zero once the due date passes with no submission, if your organization has it turned on.
  • Drop rules on a category can drop each learner's lowest grade, for example their weakest quiz.

Check, Then Release

  1. Look over the final grades in Enter Grades, and spot-check a few learners by hand.
  2. If you use the adjusted final grade, make any changes now.
  3. Release the final grades. If you chose Automatically release final grade during setup, learners already see them, so check early.

Knowledge Check

A learner was excused from one quiz because of illness. How should you record it so their grade is fair?

Wrap-Up

Finish the four knowledge checks to complete the module.

  • Set up the grade book first: grading system, final grade, and how to treat ungraded work.
  • Publish a rubric before you try to add it to an assignment.
  • Check Evaluation & Feedback whenever quiz results are not reaching learners or the grade book.
  • Check final grades before release, especially if they release automatically.
