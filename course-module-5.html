Captions and Transcripts

Captions and transcripts turn sound into text, so learners can follow without hearing the audio.

Who Uses Captions

Learners who are Deaf or hard of hearing rely on them. Many others turn them on in noisy offices, in quiet homes, or when the speaker's language is not their first.

What Good Captions Include

  • The words as spoken, in time with the audio
  • Who is speaking, when it is not obvious
  • Sounds that matter, such as an alarm or a phone ringing

Automatic Captions Need Editing

Automatic captions are a useful first draft. They often get names, technical terms and punctuation wrong, so read them through and correct them before you publish.

Transcripts

A transcript is the full text of a video or audio file, placed on the page or offered as a download. It helps learners who prefer to read, and it lets everyone search the content.
