Why Accessibility Matters

Accessible design ensures that training reaches every learner, including those whose needs were not anticipated during development.

Who Benefits

In Canada, 27% of people aged 15 and older have one or more disabilities. That is 8 million people, and some of them are your learners.

Accessibility also helps people in temporary and everyday situations:

  • A learner with low vision who enlarges the text
  • A learner with a broken wrist who uses only the keyboard this month
  • A learner on a loud train who reads captions instead of listening

What Ontario Requires

Under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA), public sector organizations, and businesses and non-profits with 50 or more employees, must make their public websites and web content meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA.

WCAG stands for Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. The rest of this course covers the parts that matter most when you build training.

Four Questions to Ask

  • Can everyone perceive it, by sight, sound or touch?
  • Can everyone operate it, with a mouse, a keyboard or a touch screen?
  • Can everyone understand it, through plain language and a predictable layout?
  • Does it work with assistive technology, such as screen readers?

Sources: Statistics Canada, Canadian Survey on Disability, 2022 and Ontario, How to make websites accessible.
