Chapter I · About

My Story

Lifelong learner and wizard on a mission to make learning fun again.

My path into instructional design began in 2014, in a secondary school classroom where I was working as a teaching assistant. I loved watching students find their purpose, learn to think critically about the world and do their very best work, and I wanted to be part of that. By 2017, I was teaching at Keewaytinook Internet High School, running a fully online program for students in North Spirit Lake First Nation. I spent my days mentoring them one-on-one as they worked through self-paced courses, and through those conversations I started to learn what really keeps a learner going when no one else is in the room.

I carried that lesson with me to York Region, where I taught for the next five years. I built my lessons around universal design for learning because I’d come to believe that every student deserved a way in, whichever way that turned out to be.

In 2023, my work grew beyond my own classroom. At North York Community House, I moved into learning experience design and led the rollout of a learning management system for more than 100 staff. I found that adults who are learning something new need what my students had always needed: clarity, support, and a reason to keep going.

Today, I’m an instructional designer at the Ontario Federation of Indigenous Friendship Centres, building curricula grounded in Indigenous teaching and learning approaches. In many ways, it brings me back to those early days with my students in North Spirit Lake.

Along the way, I’ve learned to bring together instructional design, AI-assisted workflows and data-informed iteration to create learning experiences that are engaging, accessible and built to last. It is my priority to work in partnership with others, from frontline teams to executive leadership.

Chapter II

How I Think About Learning

  • Start With the People

    Before I build anything, I talk to the learners and the people who support them. Needs analysis, personas and journey maps tell me who they are and where they get stuck.

  • Design for Everyone

    I use universal design for learning and audit every piece against AODA accessibility standards, so no learner has to ask for a workaround.

  • Make It Stick

    People learn by doing. I mix formal learning with practice, peers and real work, so new skills show up on the job, not just in a quiz.

  • Let the Data Decide

    I track participation, completion and comprehension, and use what the numbers show to improve the next version.

  • Keep It Fun

    Adult learning doesn't mean we can't have fun along the way. Casting spells is a lifelong skill.
